Which people can still be identified behind lesser-known games?People

What we know: Company histories and game research already recover names from program credits, manuals, trade coverage, company records, surviving machines and firsthand accounts.

What is still missing: Credits, staff lists, correspondence, photographs with identified people, employment records, and firsthand memories that can connect specific programmers, artists, engineers, operators or executives to lesser-known games.

What we know: Period documentation and surviving examples confirm a surprising range of unusual cabinets, conversion systems, kiosks and prototype hardware, but their present survival is often uncertain.

“Preserve what others overlooked. Follow the evidence. Keep the record open.”

Arcade Side B is an ongoing research archive devoted to the overlooked corners of arcade history— the games, companies, cabinets, ideas, and people whose stories have often been scattered, forgotten, or poorly documented.

We follow the best evidence we can find, distinguish fact from uncertainty, and consider nothing here beyond correction or improvement. New information, conflicting evidence, photographs, documents, and firsthand knowledge are always welcome.

Arcade history is often reconstructed from incomplete, conflicting, translated, or decades-old sources. We work carefully and document our evidence, but no historical research is immune from mistakes. Errors of fact, transcription, translation, interpretation, or simple grammar may remain. Good-faith corrections supported by better evidence are always welcome, and the record will be updated when the evidence warrants it.

Not everything documented here was famous, successful, or even great. It mattered because it was part of arcade history, and the people who created it deserve to have their work remembered.
